# Crit 8 reflection

I'll be direct: crit 8 didn't happen. The repo sat as the untouched starter —
one commit — until I started building for crit 9 today. There's no
crit-8-specific breakthrough to report, because there was no crit 8 work, and
I'd rather state that clearly than imply otherwise.

What I did take from falling behind is that the cost of starting late isn't
only less time to build — it's that the things which actually catch your
mistakes (running the app properly, checking the real deployed version
instead of trusting what it looks like locally, stepping back to ask whether
something genuinely works or just appears to) are the first things to get
dropped when you're racing a deadline. I only made time for that checking
today because I deliberately forced it in, and it still felt tight.

This has changed how I think about "I'll start closer to the deadline, I work
better under pressure" as something I tell myself. It isn't really a
time-management preference — it's a decision to let the crit itself be the
first real test of my work, instead of having already found and fixed the
problems before anyone else sees it. That's the standard I want to hold
myself to going forward, not just for this project.
