# Shrinking board

Added on 27 October 2026 for the shrinking board (ADR 0011, rules R61 to R69), and amended the same day after playtesting: the walls were removed and the ceiling slowed down. U68, U69 and U70 to U74 describe the ceiling only. It extends `motion-and-endings.md` (the reveal), `earthquake.md` and `tutorial.md`.

The board on screen is always drawn as 7 columns by 6 rows. It never changes size, so the phone layout cannot change (promise 4). What shrinks is the **open area**: closed cells turn into rock, one row at a time from the top.

## The warning

- **U68** The round before the ceiling drops, a warning banner (`shrink-warning`) sits on the round line above the board, beside "Round N" and sharing its row so no row is added (the 320 x 568 screen has under 20px to spare). It is shown while the round that comes right before a ceiling drop is open, that is when the round number on screen is 12 or 20 (R61). It says "The ceiling drops after this round". There is only this one message: the walls were removed (ADR 0011). At 320px **the banner is always one line** and sits on the same line as "Round N", in smaller type, so it costs no height at all (a check measures it with the widest nicknames a player can choose). It has a small crack icon, so it differs by shape as well as colour, is an `aria-live` status, has the `--outline` like every other piece in the toy style, with `--ink` text on a `--yellow` fill (the `--timer-warn` orange is kept for the flashing cells, U69). It is hidden when no drop is due, once the board is at its floor of 4 rows, and once the game is over. The browser works out when from the engine's `ceilingDue` (the one schedule), only to show the warning (R20).
- **U69** In the same round the cells that are about to close, the top open row, flash with a crack pattern (class `doomed`, `data-doomed="true"`). The flash is a crack pattern in `--timer-warn` and a slow pulse of its opacity (`--dur-doomed`). Under reduced motion the crack pattern shows and does not pulse, so the information is the same. Nothing about the flash depends on a choice: it is the same for both players (R22).

## When it happens

- **U70** When a round resolves with a ceiling drop, the top row **cracks** and **crumbles** into dust and then **turns into rock**. It starts as the round's discs land (`--ms-collapse-start`, 250ms after the drop begins) and takes `--dur-collapse` (200ms) in all, using `transform` and `opacity` only. An earthquake round still takes shake, crumble and slide (U65) first, so the longest round is about 900ms, inside the one-second promise (R23, U23). The sequence always ends with the board exactly as the server sent it. **Under reduced motion** nothing moves: the closed row simply changes to rock.
- **U71** Rock is solid and clearly not rubble. A closed cell loses its round hole and shows a square-cornered block (`data-piece` stays empty and the cell carries `data-closed="true"`) filled with `--rock`, shaded with `--rock-dark` and cut by `--ink` crack lines, with no hatch pattern. Rubble stays a round grey hatched piece in a hole, so the two differ by shape and colour (`docs/design.md`). Rock belongs to nobody. It never carries a win glow.
- **U72** A column that is full at the current number of rows cannot be chosen: its hit area is disabled (`aria-disabled="true"`), and tapping it or pressing its key says "That column is full. Pick another." (U14) and sends nothing. There are no closed columns: all 7 stay open for the whole game.
- **U73** The result line adds what closed: "The ceiling dropped. The top row turned to rock." The browser knows because the number of rows in the new state is smaller than the one it had drawn. **In a round with a ceiling drop the usual sentence only says what is still on the board**, because a disc that landed in a square that closed straight away is not in the state the server sends, and the browser must not claim a round was missed when it cannot tell. So it lists each disc or rubble block that stayed ("Your disc landed in column 3." and "Your opponent's disc landed in column 5." or "Crash: rubble landed."), never "missed the round" or "chose the same column" for a piece that is not there, and when nothing stayed it gives only the shrink sentence.
- **U74** A tutorial step "The board shrinks" after the Earthquake step. A warning banner and a flashing top row show on the practice board, and the player is asked to tap any column. Their disc drops and the top row cracks, crumbles and turns to rock. Caption: "The board shrinks as rounds pass: after round 12 the ceiling drops, and it drops again after round 20. Pieces in a closed row crumble away." The step follows the rules of U41 to U48 (it waits for the player, Next appears only once it is done, it works by tap and keyboard, and under reduced motion it still makes sense). The practice board never has a piece in its top row, as in U41. Later steps move down by one (see `tutorial.md`).

## Test hooks

`shrink-warning`, `result-line` (U73), and on every cell `data-closed` and `data-doomed`. In the tutorial: `tutorial-warning`.

## Layout cost

- **U75** Everything the shrinking board adds must leave the first-screen rule of `docs/design.md` true at 320 x 568 and the marking viewports, in the worst case. At 320px the opponent's panel shows its two power-ups as two small icons on one line (the shield and the Earthquake, each struck through when used), with the words "available" or "used" kept as visually hidden text for screen readers and for the test hooks `opponent-shield` and `opponent-earthquake`. The words took a second line and cost 35px that the banner needs. A used power-up differs by shape (struck through) as well as colour.
